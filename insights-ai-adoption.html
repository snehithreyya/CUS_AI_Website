AI & Data

From Pilot to Production: A Realistic Path to Enterprise AI

CUS Solution Team· August 14, 2026· 7 min read

Enterprise AI has a graveyard problem. Roughly four out of five pilots generate an encouraging demo and then quietly stall — never reaching the users, systems, or scale where they'd actually create value. The reason is almost never the model. It's everything around the model: messy data, no way to measure whether the output is good, and no one who owns it once the excitement fades.

Shipping AI to production is a discipline, not a breakthrough. Here is the stage-gated path we use to get from pilot to something that runs, earns trust, and survives contact with real users.

1. Select use cases by value and tractability

Not every problem deserves AI, and the flashiest use case is rarely the right first one. Score candidates on two axes: business value if it works, and how tractable it is given your data and risk tolerance. Start where a modest, reliable win builds credibility — then reinvest that trust in harder problems.

2. Get honest about data readiness

Most stalls trace back here. Before building, ask whether the data exists, whether you're allowed to use it, whether it's clean and current, and whether it reflects the real-world cases you'll face. A pilot on curated sample data that collapses on production inputs isn't progress — it's a mirage.

3. Define evaluation before you build

If you can't measure quality, you can't ship responsibly. Decide up front what "good" means, assemble a representative test set, and evaluate every change against it. Pair automated metrics with human review for the cases that matter. Evaluation is what turns "it seems to work" into evidence you can stand behind.

  • Build a representative evaluation set early and keep it versioned.
  • Track quality, latency, and cost together — a great answer that's too slow or expensive doesn't ship.
  • Keep a human in the loop for high-stakes outputs until the data earns your trust.

4. Plan governance and ownership from day one

Production AI needs an owner, guardrails, and a monitoring plan — for accuracy drift, cost, and misuse. Decide who is accountable, what the model is and isn't allowed to do, and how you'll catch regressions. Governance isn't bureaucracy; it's what lets you move fast without breaking trust.

Key takeaways

  • →Most pilots stall on data, evaluation, and ownership — not the model itself.
  • →Choose first use cases by value and tractability; earn trust with a reliable win.
  • →Be honest about data readiness before building anything.
  • →Define evaluation up front, and assign clear ownership and governance for production.
